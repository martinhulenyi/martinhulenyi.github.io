## Abstract
Does spatial isolation contribute to the rise of Chega, Portugal’s populist far-right party? We use road-based market potential as an inverse measure of isolation for mainland municipalities. To address endogeneity, we instrument 2021 market potential with its 1971 and 1864 counterparts. A one standard deviation decrease in the log of market potential raises Chega’s vote share in the 2025
parliamentary election by 2.7-3.4 percentage points. The effect grows as the party consolidates, extends to presidential elections, is weaker for European elections, and null for municipal elections. It operates through comparisons with the rest of the country, not with neighbouring municipalities.

## Motivation 
- Role of asymmetries in accessibility to economic markets as a driver of discontent remains largely understudied.
- Recent emergence of righ-wing populist party in Portugal, Chega, despite Portugal's massive constructiond and reconstruction of road transport infrastructure in the past 50 years.


## Research question
- Do persistent differences in access to markets of by road impact the support for Chega (the far right party in Portugal) on municipal level?


## Contribution
- Evaluation of the causal impact of market potential on support for the far-right.
- Analysis of a variety of elections since the establishment of Chega, allowing to see the heterogenity across election types as well as across years. 
## Empirical strategy
- Evaluating causal impact of market potential in 2021, instrumented by its lagged values on Chega's vote share in various elections.
- **Dependent variable**: vote share of Chega on municipal level.  
- **Variables of interest**: market potential of a municipality in 2021.
- **Instrumental variables**: market potential of a municipality in 1971 and 1864 along with the road density of a municipality in 1945 and 1750.
- **Control variables**: mainly various geographical and historical data, additionally with contemporaneous data in some specifications. 
- **Method**: OLS and 2SLS.

## Result
- Lower market potential significantly increases the support for Chega in 2025
parliamentary elections: a one standard deviation decrease in the log of market
potential raises Chega’s vote share by 2.7–3.4 percentage points.
- The effect is not statistically significant for 2019 parliamentary elections and grows with the emergence of Chega as a credible vehicle for discontent.
- The pattern extends to presidential elections, but is weaker in elections to the
European Parliament and absent in municipal elections. 
- The effect of relative MP is substantial on the non-local level, while not significant on the local level.
